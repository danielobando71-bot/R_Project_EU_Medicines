EU authorised medicines analysis per therapeutic area

Author

Daniel Obando

1 Business understanding

Knowing where regulatory approvals concentrate shows which disease areas receive the most pharmaceutical development and EMA attention. This helps identify where treatment options are abundant and where they are scarce.

1.1 Determine Business Objectives

Background: the European Medicines Agency (EMA) publishes a dataset of every medicine assessed centrally in the EU, including its status, therapeutic area (MeSH), regulatory pathway and authorization dates. Understanding where authorized medicines concentrate helps identify which disease areas receive the most pharmaceutical development.

Business objective (research question/hypothesis): identify which therapeutic areas have the highest number of currently authorized human medicines in the EU.

Related questions:

1- Which characteristics are most common in the top areas?

2- Among currently authorized medicines, how is the distribution of the top areas by year of marketing authorization.

Business success criteria:

Success for this project is accomplished if it : delivers (a) a ranked list of the top 10 therapeutic areas with the most authorizations by the EMA, (b) a clear profile of what the top areas have in common, and (c) a visualization understandable by a non-scientific reader.

1.2 Assessing the situation

Inventory of resources:

  • EMA data-set (Excel, 2,748 records, 39 columns, downloaded 11/10/2026)

  • RStudio

  • Tidyverse, Quarto, Git

Assumptions about the data:

  • Currency: data provided by the EMA contains accurate and up to date information about all listed medicines.

  • Data integrity: provided attributes for each medicine are officially validated.

  • A medicine is considered as currently available in the European market only if the column “Medicine Status” is equal to “Authorised”.

  • Every authorised medicine have the same weight in the analysis, regardless of sales volume or clinical impact.

1.3 Determining Data Mining Goals

Goal: count and rank therapeutic areas by number of authorised human medicines, then describe the top areas.

Problem type: data description and summarization, with some segmentation by area.

Technical success criteria: the counts are reproducible and every filtering and counting decision is documented in the code.

1.4 Produce Project Plan

Follow the six CRISP-DM phases to answer the question “Which therapeutic areas have the highest number of currently authorized human medicines in the EU?”.

For achieving this goal, RStudio is going to be used to develop a Quarto Report, using the following tools: readxl, dplyr, tidyr, lubridate, ggplot2 and Git.

3 Data preparation

3.1 Select the data

Data selection was guided by three criteria defined in CRISP-DM: relevance to the data mining goals, data quality, and technical constraints. Selection was applied to both records (rows) and attributes (columns).

3.1.1 Rationale for inclusion/exclusion

Veterinary medicines and human medicines with a status other than “Authorised” were excluded, as the business question concerns currently authorised human medicines.

Initially, the inclusion of refused medicines was considered, in order to compare them with authorised ones. However, the number of refused medicines is very low compared with the number of authorised medicines (65 vs. 1,568). It is considered that this imbalance makes it difficult to carry out a meaningful analysis, so refused medicines were excluded.

Of the 39 original variables, only those needed for the three questions were kept: therapeutic area, authorisation date, medicine (Generic, Biosimilar )and regulatory related characteristics (Advanced therapy, PRIME: priority medicine).

3.2 Clean the data

3.2.1 Removing missing values

Records with missing values in any of the selected variables were removed using na.omit(). This reduced the working dataset from 1,568 to 1,532 records (36 records, 2.3%). Of these, 13 lacked a therapeutic area, 6 lacked a marketing authorisation date, and 17 had missing values in Marketing authorisation developer / applicant / holder. Since the removed records represent a small share of the dataset, their impact on the results is considered minimal.

3.2.2 Normalize text per area

As shown in Phase 2, some records contained multiple therapeutic areas separated by semicolons (;). These values were split into individual therapeutic areas to allow each area to be counted separately.

As dates in Marketing authorisation date are formatted as text, part of the cleaning consist in transforming those dates into a “date” type object. For this, we call the package library(lubridate), using the function as_date().

Generated records

One single medicine can belong to several therapeutic areas separated by ;, so counting areas requires one record per medicine area. Using separate_rows(), a new table, medicines_areas, was generated. Each record keeps all the variables of its medicine, including authorisation_year, so the same table supports both the ranking of areas and their evolution over the years.

4 Modelling / analysis

4.1 Select modeling technique

The “model” used is a set of descriptive techniques, implemented with dplyr and ggplot2, using frequency counts, proportions by group and time-series counts.

Question Technique Visualisation
Main: which therapeutic areas have the most authorised medicines? Frequency count of medicines per area, ranked in descending order Horizontal bar chart of the top 10 areas.
Supporting 1: which characteristics are repeated in the leading areas? Count and share of “Yes” vs. “No” for each characteristic (generic, biosimilar, PRIME, advanced therapy) within each area, compared with all authorised medicines Stacked bar charts, one panel per characteristic.
Supporting 2: how has the pattern changed over the years? Count of medicines per authorisation year in the top 4 areas, over the last 20 years Line chart (one line per area)

4.2 Why these techniques suit the questions?

  • The main question asks how many, so counting and ranking answers it directly, with no further transformation.

  • The second question asks which characteristics are repeated. A count alone would be misleading because areas differ in size, so shares within each area are used. Comparing them with the share in all authorised medicines shows whether a characteristic is really more frequent in the leading areas.

  • The third question asks about change over time, which requires a time series. Yearly counts keep the resolution needed to see peaks, and the line chart allows the trajectory of each area to be followed in one plot.

5 Evaluation

5.1 Evaluate the results

5.1.1 Top 10 therapeutic areas

The distribution of authorised medicines by therapeutic area (Figure 1) reveals a distinct concentration in oncology and chronic diseases. Breast Neoplasms records the highest number of authorisations with 65, followed by Diabetes Mellitus, Type 2 (59) and HIV Infections (57). At the lower end of the top 10, Cancer registers 31 authorisations.

5.1.2 Characteristics of the top areas

To further examine the regulatory features of these treatments, the top 10 therapeutic areas were disaggregated by designation type (Figure 2). While categories such as PRIME and Advanced therapy account for a minimal or negligible proportion of authorised medicines across these leading areas, Biosimilars constitute a substantial share within rheumatological conditions (such as Arthritis, Rheumatoid and Arthritis, Psoriatic). Additionally, Generics maintain a steady presence across several leading chronic and infectious conditions, particularly HIV Infections and Breast Neoplasms.

Consequently, given the results observed for PRIME and Advanced therapies, it can be concluded that these specific features are highly unlikely to be seen among the most extensively studied and established medicines in the market.

5.1.3 Trend of the top 4 areas over the years

To evaluate how the distribution of leading therapeutic approvals has evolved and to identify potential temporal patterns, the annual number of authorised medicines was tracked across the top four areas (Figure 3). The data demonstrate a clear shift in research momentum over time: while Diabetes Mellitus, Type 2 led earlier approvals and HIV Infections experienced a sharp surge around 2016, Breast Neoplasms has emerged as the most consistently high volume area in recent years.

Although monthly seasonality cannot be inferred from annual resolution, the trajectories reveal distinct multi year cyclical waves (3 to 5 year intervals) rather than uniform growth. For clinical research groups, these cyclical surges suggest that R&D productivity and regulatory submissions in these major fields move in synchronized waves, which should be factored into long term strategic planning and pipeline forecasting.

5.2 Review process

Limitations found during the analysis:

  • Missing or Incomplete Data: Certain critical columns, such as “Start of evaluation date,” are entirely empty. Having access to this information would have enabled valuable insights regarding the average review timeline and waiting periods associated with each therapeutic area prior to approval.
  • Small Sample Sizes: Data concerning refused medicines was insufficient in volume, precluding its inclusion in the main comparative analysis.
  • Inconsistent Therapeutic Area Classification: Therapeutic areas suffer from granularity inconsistencies; broad categories (such as “Cancer”) coexist alongside highly specific oncological subcategories (such as “Breast neoplasms,” “Carcinoma,” and “Multiple myeloma”). For future analyses, establishing standardized and cohesive classification rules for therapeutic areas is recommended to ensure robust grouping.

6 Deployment and communication

6.1 Summary of the analysis

This project set out to analyze the European Union’s landscape of authorized human medicines, focusing on which therapeutic areas lead the market and how their regulatory and temporal profiles have evolved. By examining approval amounts, shared features, and multi year trends, several key insights can be obtained, resulting in direct strategic value for industry stakeholders, healthcare analysts, national health agencies and clinical research groups:

  • Concentration in Chronic Conditions and Oncology: The analysis reveals that medicines are heavily concentrated in specific fields, with Breast Neoplasms, Diabetes Mellitus (Type 2), and HIV Infections leading the market.

  • Specialized Designations are Niche: When investigating product characteristics, Biosimilars show a strong presence primarily within rheumatological conditions (such as rheumatoid and psoriatic arthritis). However, special regulatory pathways like PRIME and Advanced Therapies account for a minimal share of these leading areas. This indicates that these features are highly specialized and unlikely to be found among the most mainstream medicines.

  • Cyclical Waves Over Strict Seasonality: Tracking approvals over time demonstrates that R&D output and regulatory submissions do not grow uniformly. Instead, they move in distinct multi year cycles (waves lasting 3 to 5 years).

6.2 Strategic Takeaway for Stakeholders

Understanding these long term cyclical waves and the specific boundaries of regulatory features allows clinical research groups and decision makers to better anticipate market shifts, align pipeline development with high impact therapeutic areas, and improve long-term strategic forecasting.
